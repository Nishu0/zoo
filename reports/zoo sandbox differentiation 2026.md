# Three tricks zoo keeps to itself

As of October 2026, **yes — zoo is doing at least three things that no surveyed commercial provider or open-source runtime documents**: warm pools whose containers boot already carrying their final sandbox identity (name, home volume, guest credentials pre-provisioned at pool time), batch execution of up to 25 commands over a single HTTP round trip with `stop_on_error`, and macOS sandboxes with memory saved-state resume served behind an API — the last with the **only public restore-latency figure in the category (~9.7 s for 8 GB)**. A fourth cluster — a dial-out guest agent riding one persistent websocket with zero inbound listeners and per-command HMAC tokens — is unmatched among AI-sandbox vendors but mirrors the decades-old CI-runner pattern, and the remaining claims (deny-by-default host-side egress, multi-kind API, single-laptop self-hosting) are ahead of the field rather than alone in it. The timing amplifies the value of every one of these: the category is consolidating around OpenSandbox as the open-source benchmark while **Runloop winds down on November 13, 2026**, **Daytona abandoned its open-source repo in June 2026**, **OpenAI bought the Tart team in April 2026**, and **MacVisor shipped its first public beta on October 16, 2026**. Two honesty caveats frame everything that follows: seven of eleven major providers simply do not document their transport or pool internals, so several "nobody" verdicts rest on absence of evidence — and one widely repeated figure about OpenSandbox's speed (12–16 ms creates) is wrong against its own published docs by a factor of four to five.

## A shakeout month: rivals exit while Apple-silicon agents heat up

The competitive map moved decisively in the six months before this audit, mostly in zoo's favor. **Runloop is shutting down**: its docs state plainly that "Runloop is winding down its services. Support for existing customers continues through November 13, 2026 at 11:59 PM PT" ([Runloop docs](https://docs.runloop.ai/docs/overview/what-is-runloop)). **Daytona, the previous open-source poster child, closed its code**: "As of June 2026, Daytona's core development has moved to a private codebase. This repository will receive no further updates, fixes, or releases" ([daytonaio/daytona](https://github.com/daytonaio/daytona)), a move announced June 11, 2026 ([Northflank](https://northflank.com/blog/top-daytona-io-alternatives-for-running-ai-code-in-secure-sandboxed-environments)), with community members noting the self-hosting option was effectively withdrawn ([dev.to](https://dev.to/rohittcodes/my-journey-with-daytona-and-how-i-plan-to-use-it-going-forward-22bb)). Most strategically, **OpenAI acquired Cirrus Labs — the makers of Tart — on April 7, 2026**, folding the team into its Agent Infrastructure group, re-releasing Tart/Vetu/Orchard under more permissive licenses, and shutting Cirrus CI on June 1 ([cirruslabs.org](https://cirruslabs.org); [MacStadium blog](https://macstadium.com/blog/cirrus-labs-joining-openai)). The Tart repo now lives at [github.com/openai/tart](https://github.com/openai/tart) (release 2.41.1, October 15, 2026), and community commentary reads it as OpenAI wanting "something similar [to WSL2] for Codex on Mac" ([HN #47730194](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47730194)) — unconfirmed, but a clear watch item for anyone whose differentiation depends on macOS virtualization.

On the same lane, **MacVisor (ScaleNinja) entered public beta on October 16, 2026** as the only Virtualization.framework tool found that does live disk+memory snapshots *and* suspend/resume for both macOS and Linux guests, with instant linked clones — but as a local, single-Mac desktop/CLI app requiring a macOS 27 host, with the caveat that "suspended VMs and live snapshots are only meant to restore on the build that made them" ([ScaleNinja blog](https://scaleninja.com/blog/macvisor); [MacVisor vs Orka](https://scaleninja.com/macvisor/vs/orka-desktop)). Meanwhile Scrapybara — historically the only vendor shipping code + browser + desktop instances under one API — pivoted away from virtual desktops entirely "to becoming a dedicated AGI research lab" ([scrapybara.com](https://scrapybara.com)). Cloudflare's Containers + Sandbox SDK hit GA on April 13, 2026 ([changelog](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-04-13-containers-sandbox-ga)), and OpenSandbox shipped its unified 1.1.0 release on September 21, 2026 ([release notes](https://github.com/opensandbox-group/OpenSandbox/releases/tag/release-1.1.0)).

**Correction on OpenSandbox's figures**: the widely repeated claim that OpenSandbox achieves 12–16 ms pool-admission creates, 35 ms snapshot restores, and 0.7 ms reflink rootfs prep **does not match its current public documentation**. Its performance doc (measured 2026-10-08 on a two-node ACK cluster, Firecracker 1.16.1) reports **SDK create p50 64.0 ms** warm-serial (p99 108.3 ms; 125.4 ms p99 at 10 concurrent), runtime create 33.5–50 ms p50, rootfs reflink prep **~2.3 ms p50**, and Firecracker VM-state load **0.233 ms p50** with a hot template cache ([OpenSandbox performance.md](https://github.com/opensandbox-group/OpenSandbox/blob/main/docs/architecture/fast-sandbox/performance.md); [README](https://github.com/opensandbox-group/OpenSandbox/blob/main/README.md) — an older README snapshot said 97 ms p50). The "12–16 ms" figures could not be located in any fetched public source and should be treated as unverified; 64 ms p50 is the number to benchmark against. Two research-quality notes apply to this entire report: one researcher's web-search tooling was non-functional, so findings came from direct primary-source fetches on 2026-10-22, and negative findings for several vendors mean "searched, not found" rather than verified absence.

## Three mechanisms nobody else documents

**Verdict table for all seven capabilities**, then the evidence for the three unique ones:

| zoo capability | Verdict | Closest found |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Warm pool boots containers already carrying final sandbox identity | **GENUINELY UNIQUE** | OpenSandbox pre-provisions network slots + policy bindings, not identity |
| 2. Dial-out agent, one websocket, no inbound listeners, per-command HMAC | **RARE/PARTIAL** (HMAC tokens: unique; dial-out: unique in-category as documented) | CI-runner agent pattern; all documented AI-sandbox transports dial in |
| 3. macOS VMs: clonefile clone, save-state stop, 9.7 s restore, API-first | **RARE/PARTIAL** overall; saved-state-resume-via-API is **GENUINELY UNIQUE** | MacVisor (local beta), Tart (CLI, macOS guests only), Daytona/use.computer (reserved Mac) |
| 4. Batch exec: ≤25 commands, one round trip, stop_on_error | **GENUINELY UNIQUE** | Persistent shell sessions (Daytona); streaming single-exec everywhere |
| 5. Deny-by-default egress enforced host-side | **RARE/PARTIAL** | OpenSandbox (fail-closed, guest-immutable); E2B (host-side but allow-by-default) |
| 6. Code + desktop (VNC) + browser kinds under one API | **GENUINELY UNIQUE** among active vendors | Scrapybara (exited the category); desktop/browser options exist scattered |
| 7. Whole platform self-hosted on one laptop, pluggable backends | **RARE/PARTIAL** | Microsandbox (single binary, microVM-only, repo recently transferred) |

**Identity-at-pool-boot warm pools** — Pre-warmed capacity is table stakes: Daytona documents warm pools with claim-on-create and background replenishment ([Warm Pools docs](https://www.daytona.io/docs/en/warm-pools/)), and OpenSandbox makes create "a constant-time admission into capacity that already exists" ([scheduling.md](https://github.com/opensandbox-group/OpenSandbox/blob/main/docs/architecture/fast-sandbox/scheduling.md)). But every surveyed system pre-provisions only *capacity and network slots* at pool time. OpenSandbox ships "the attachment identity plus policy bindings … with the admission" while the sandbox entrypoint "is delivered as a task to the sandbox rather than baked into the pod, which is what allows a pre-warmed Fastlet to serve arbitrary templates" — deliberately identity-free slots. E2B creates sandboxes by restoring identity-less snapshots and binds identity "after resume via control plane" ([E2B ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/e2b-dev/infra/blob/main/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)); its client-side SDK pools (OSEP-0005, OSEP-0021) pre-prepare whole sandboxes, but in client SDKs, one sandbox per slot ([OSEP-0005](https://github.com/opensandbox-group/OpenSandbox/blob/main/oseps/0005-client-side-sandbox-pool.md)). **No public production or open-source system was found that boots pooled containers already carrying their final name, home volume, and guest credentials.** Zoo's design also matches the confirmed direction of travel — OpenSandbox's stated invariant "policy delivery can be late, never early-open" is the same fail-closed pattern zoo uses when handing secrets and egress policy over at claim time — but the identity-at-boot part appears undocumented anywhere. Caveat: pool internals for Morph, Blaxel, Hopx, AgentSphere, and even Daytona's pre-creation method are unpublished, and E2B/Modal/Blaxel may run internal pools they don't document.

**Batch exec over one round trip** — Across eleven-plus providers and their documented API surfaces, **every exec endpoint is one command per request**: E2B (`commands.run` per call — [SDK reference](https://e2b.dev/docs/sdk-reference/python-sdk/v1.0.6/sandbox_async)), Cloudflare (a full Commands API of `exec`/`execStream`/process handlers with no batch method — [Cloudflare Commands API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/sandbox/sdk/api/commands/)), Runloop ("the primary method of executing commands is the exec command" — [Runloop docs](https://docs.runloop.ai/docs/devboxes/execute-commands)), Modal, TensorLake, OpenSandbox, Morph, Daytona (which offers persistent *Sessions* instead — [Daytona Process docs](https://www.daytona.io/docs/en/process-code-execution/)). The nearest analogues are E2B's batch *fork* (N forks per call) and OpenSandbox's batch sandbox *creation* — neither is multi-command execution. Vendors amortize round trips with streaming (SSE/WebSocket) and session reuse, not declarative ordered batches; shell `&&` chaining, the universal fallback, collapses per-command exit-code capture and offers no API-level stop_on_error. The honest framing: the *idea* has deep prior art outside the category (AWS SSM RunCommand, CI workflow steps — background knowledge, not re-verified this session), and Blaxel/Morph/CodeSandbox full API surfaces were not exhaustively enumerated, but its application to a sandbox exec API appears unclaimed as of October 2026.

**macOS saved-state resume behind an API** — macOS agent sandboxes barely exist: Daytona's macOS offering is a third-party platform (use.computer) where "each sandbox runs on a Mac **reserved for your account**" — static allocation, no documented suspend ([Daytona macOS docs](https://www.daytona.io/docs/en/macos-sandboxes)); MacStadium Orka explicitly does not support stop/start/suspend/resume on Apple silicon nodes ("Intel-only operations due to Apple" — [Orka docs](https://docs.macstadium.com/orka/orka-resources/apple-silicon-based-support)); Anka 3's ARM suspend is GUI-only, with CLI suspend silently performing a full stop "due to an inability to move a suspended VM from one host to another (Apple limitation)" ([Anka release notes](https://docs.veertu.com/anka/anka-virtualization-cli/release-notes)); Tart supports suspend for macOS guests only, with live snapshots still a requested feature ([openai/tart #796](https://github.com/openai/tart/issues/796), [#147](https://github.com/openai/tart/issues/147)); and Cua's cloud tier is described by a third-party comparison as "Linux amd64 only; no suspend; 15-minute claim TTL" ([Simular.ai](https://www.simular.ai/alternatives/cloud-computers-for-ai-agents)) — though Cua's own marketing advertises macOS fleets on Apple Silicon ([cua.ai](https://cua.ai/)), a conflict the researchers could not resolve. **No surveyed provider performs memory saved-state suspend/resume on macOS in any API product**; MacVisor has the capability but only as a local single-Mac beta. Zoo's **~25 s save / ~9.7 s restore at 8 GB** (internal benchmark) is the only public number of its kind — nobody else publishes macOS save-state restore latencies at all ([macOS landscape notes](https://scaleninja.com/blog/macvisor)). The individual primitives are parity — APFS clonefile cloning is exactly how Tart's `tart clone` works ([Clone.swift](https://github.com/openai/tart/blob/main/Sources/tart/Commands/Clone.swift)) and save-state suspend ships in Tart ([Suspend.swift](https://github.com/openai/tart/blob/main/Sources/tart/Commands/Suspend.swift)) — but productizing resume-from-save-state as the create path for agent sandboxes, plus fresh SSH host keys per clone (no other provider documents re-identification; AWS scrubs hosts and GitLab deletes VMs instead — [AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/mac-instance-stop.html)), has no equivalent. Demand-side evidence confirms the gap: a February 2026 first-person account "was, unfortunately, unable to find any Mac solution that met my requirements" for a safe agent sandbox ([Kevin Lynagh](https://kevinlynagh.com/newsletter/2026_02_01_vibe)).

## Dial-out transport: differentiated in-category, but the evidence has holes

Every provider that documents its transport uses **dial-in**: an in-sandbox daemon awaits connections from the platform. E2B's envd "exposes an E2B-compatible REST and gRPC API on port 49983" that the SDK and proxy connect *into* ([envd README](https://github.com/e2b-dev/runtime/blob/main/packages/envd/README.md); [ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/e2b-dev/infra/blob/main/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)); TensorLake routes "through the sandbox proxy to the daemon inside the sandbox" ([TensorLake API](https://docs.tensorlake.ai/api-reference/v2/introduction)); Cloudflare's Durable Object "runs commands with the Container API" ([sandbox-sdk](https://github.com/cloudflare/sandbox-sdk)); OpenSandbox funnels "inbound traffic through a unified ingress gateway" to its in-sandbox execd ([OpenSandbox README](https://github.com/opensandbox-group/OpenSandbox/blob/main/README.md)); Cua execs through cua-spacesd on port 3211 ([Cua docs](https://cua.ai/docs/llms-full.txt)). **No AI-sandbox vendor documents the reverse design** — a guest agent dialing out and holding the control channel. That makes zoo's architecture structurally different from everything published in its category, but it cannot be claimed as absolutely unique: transport direction is undocumented at Daytona, Modal, Morph, Blaxel, Runloop, CodeSandbox, and Replit — "the single largest evidence gap in the checklist" — and the dial-out pattern is standard, long-standing practice among CI runners (GitHub Actions runner, Buildkite agent, AWS SSM agent — background knowledge the researchers could not re-verify against primary docs this session because the vendor pages are JS-rendered). Two of zoo's companion properties hold up better: **zero inbound control listeners** is matched by no checked vendor (E2B actively sells inbound public URLs — [public URL docs](https://docs.e2b.dev/network/public-url) — which is also why competitors ship inbound: zoo's posture breaks "preview my dev server" workflows and should be positioned as a deliberate tradeoff), and **per-command HMAC guest tokens have no found equivalent anywhere** among E2B, Daytona, Cloudflare, Runloop, Blaxel, CodeSandbox, or Microsandbox ([api surface notes](https://docs.e2b.dev/iam/workload-identity)). For calibration, Wasmer's September 2026 benchmark measured exec round trips of **82.8 ms on E2B and 103.3 ms on Modal** versus 5.6 ms locally ([Wasmer RESULTS-2026-09-01](https://github.com/wasmerio/wasmer-sdk/blob/main/benchmarks/sandboxes/RESULTS-2026-09-01.md) — vendor-published, no independent replication) — exactly the RTT-dominated cost that zoo's single-websocket transport plus 25-command batching attacks. One genuinely novel design point is undocumented anywhere in the survey: how dial-out interacts with snapshot resume, since a restored VM must re-establish its outbound channel with fresh identity.

## Security posture and platform shape: ahead, not alone

**Egress (verdict: RARE/PARTIAL).** Host-side, guest-opaque egress enforcement is no longer rare: E2B inspects SNI/Host at a firewall and proxies outbound TCP through a customer-operated SOCKS5 proxy "applied on the host after allowOut and denyOut so code inside the sandbox cannot see or route around it," failing closed when the proxy is unreachable ([E2B internet access](https://docs.e2b.dev/network/internet-access); [changelog](https://docs.e2b.dev/changelog)); Cloudflare controls egress from the Worker with `enableInternet: false` ([sandbox-sdk](https://github.com/cloudflare/sandbox-sdk)); TensorLake updates allow/deny lists on running sandboxes ([TensorLake SDK](https://docs.tensorlake.ai/sandboxes/sdk-reference)); and OpenSandbox gives guests no NET_ADMIN with "enforcement rules … invisible and immutable from inside the guest" ([OpenSandbox README](https://github.com/opensandbox-group/OpenSandbox/blob/main/README.md)). Zoo's remaining edge is the **default posture**: E2B is explicitly allow-by-default ("Every sandbox has outbound access to the internet by default"), while zoo denies until the owner assigns policy — a posture OpenSandbox's fail-closed invariant ("a sandbox is never reachable before its policy is") closely matches, and which industry observers note is emerging among consumers of sandboxes rather than vendors ([WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/agent-sandbox-egress-defaults)). Claim the combination — deny-at-birth + guest-immutable + no inbound listeners + HMAC per command — not the enforcement point alone. **Multi-kind API (verdict: unique among active vendors).** No active vendor ships code-execution, desktop (VNC), and browser sandboxes as sibling kinds under one API: browser-first vendors (Kernel, Browserbase — which argues you can't just run Chromium in a code sandbox, [Browserbase blog](https://www.browserbase.com/blog/why-you-can-t-just-run-chromium-in-a-sandbox)) ship no code kind, code-first vendors (E2B, Modal, Cloudflare) ship no desktop streaming, and Scrapybara, the one historical match, exited the category ([docs](https://docs.scrapybara.com/)). The pieces exist scattered — E2B Desktop (Linux), Cua Fleet, Daytona Computer Use — so the durable claim is the *single API over three backends*, best positioned as infrastructure rather than "desktops for agents," since Scrapybara's pivot suggests standalone agent desktops were commercially hard. **Single-laptop self-hosting (verdict: RARE/PARTIAL).** Microsandbox is the confirmed single-binary, local-first platform ("runs locally and rootless using libkrun microVMs" — [microsandbox.dev](https://microsandbox.dev/)), but it is microVM-only with no macOS-guest or Docker-backend story, and its repo now appears under a new org ([superradcompany/microsandbox](https://github.com/superradcompany/microsandbox)), raising continuity questions. E2B is open-source but a multi-service Terraform/Nomad deployment whose own issue trackers show demand for single-node self-hosting ([infra #864](https://github.com/e2b-dev/infra/issues/864)); Cloudflare is structurally platform-bound; Daytona's OSS is gone. Zoo's exact shape — full API, gateway, and control plane on one Mac, with Docker and macOS-VM as pluggable backends and loopback latencies — has no confirmed equivalent.

## What to claim publicly, and what to fix first

| Public claim | Evidence strength | Fix before claiming |
|---|---|---|
| "Warm pools booted with final sandbox identity — creates are constant-time admissions" | No public design matches | Publish claim-time handoff latency and how secrets are scoped after claim; avoid absolute "nobody" wording given unpublished pool internals at 5+ vendors |
| "25 commands, one round trip, stop_on_error" | No equivalent found at 11+ providers | Publish loopback RTT vs. 25 sequential calls; contrast with session/streaming workarounds explicitly |
| "macOS sandboxes with 9.7 s saved-state resume" | Only public number in the category | Publish methodology (zoo's figures are internal benchmarks); state the Apple portability limitation and per-clone SSH re-keying story |
| "No inbound listeners; per-command HMAC tokens" | Unique as documented; CI-runner precedent exists | Frame as category-first among AI sandboxes, not world-first; explain the preview-URL tradeoff |
| "Deny-by-default egress enforced outside the sandbox" | OpenSandbox and E2B are close on enforcement | Claim the *combination* (deny-at-birth + guest-immutable + zero inbound + HMAC), not the mechanism |
| "Whole platform on one laptop" | Microsandbox is the nearest neighbor | Re-verify Microsandbox's post-transfer maintenance before head-to-head claims |

Beyond the table, four fixes matter. **First, convert internal numbers into public claims**: every zoo latency (~25 s save, ~9.7 s restore, ~69 s cold clone-to-running) is an internal benchmark, and OpenSandbox's percentile-with-methodology performance doc is now the standard competitors are measured against — a zoo equivalent for the macOS path would be the only one in existence. **Second, close the cheapest evidence gaps**: resolve the Cua cloud-macOS conflict (cua.ai marketing vs. Simular.ai's "no suspend"), check Microsandbox's egress defaults and secrets mechanism, and re-verify the CI-runner dial-out precedents against primary docs so the transport story can be told with citations. **Third, address warm-pool elasticity head-on**: OpenSandbox's own data shows pool exhaustion is the real availability risk of warm-pool designs (51/100 successes at concurrency 20 on a 20-slot pool — [performance.md](https://github.com/opensandbox-group/OpenSandbox/blob/main/docs/architecture/fast-sandbox/performance.md)), so zoo should document its replenishment and saturation behavior before a competitor raises it. **Fourth, treat all claims as perishable**: Runloop's wind-down, Daytona's closure, and MacVisor's launch all landed within six months, and OpenAI's Agent Infrastructure group now owns Tart — the most plausible path to zoo's macOS lane being contested runs through OpenAI internalizing exactly this stack. The researchers' known unverified areas, stated plainly: use.computer's full feature set (site unfetchable), the exact Apple save-state API surface behind VZ suspend ("AVMSaveState" remains unconfirmed), Apple's current SLA clause text on VM instances per Mac, EC2 Mac/Orka snapshot capabilities on Apple silicon (Orka's Intel-only lifecycle page is confirmed live), Anthropic/OpenAI first-party sandbox tool APIs (not surveyed), and all Wasmer benchmark numbers (vendor-published).

## Conclusion

The audit's clearest finding is that zoo's genuine novelty lives at the layer the rest of the industry just standardized away: not snapshot resume, not warm pools, not host-side egress — all now table stakes — but the *identity and policy binding* around them. Booting pooled sandboxes already carrying their final name, volume, and credentials; handing secrets and egress policy over in parallel at claim time; and amortizing the agent's tool loop into a single 25-command round trip are all attacks on the same bottleneck that OpenSandbox's phase timings expose — with snapshot loading at 0.233 ms, orchestration and readiness *are* the create path. Zoo's second durable asset is lane ownership: a self-hosted, API-first macOS sandbox with memory saved-state resume has no competitor, incumbent or startup, and the simultaneous exits of Runloop and open-source Daytona leave the "run the whole thing yourself" market nearly empty. The strategic risk inverts the usual one: it is not that someone copies zoo's tricks, but that OpenAI — now Tart's steward and demonstrably interested in Mac-based agent isolation — builds the macOS lane in-house, and that zoo's "nobody does this" claims decay as the remaining undocumented vendors publish. The right posture is aggressive specificity: publish methodologies and numbers now, claim combinations rather than mechanisms, and re-verify the competitive map quarterly.
